\section{Opening}
This document summarizes the results of a mass flow experiment from the first lab.
This experiment is conducted with the P.A. Hilton air conditiong unit. 
Three methods were used to determine the mass flow rate of air through the duct. 
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item Experimental integration of the pitot--measured velocity
                profile, \( \dot m_{\text{exp}} \).
          \item Manufacturer correlation, \( \dot m_{\text{man}} \).
          \item Obstruction--meter (orifice) theory, \( \dot m_{\text{obs}} \).
        \end{enumerate}
For the three methods, Klin-McClintock(KM) first-order analytical method and Monte Carlo(MC) are used
to determine uncertainty propagation.

\section{Summary}
Experimental integration has the largest relative uncertainty. The rest are very small.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Central values from the code (example numbers):
        \begin{align*}
          \dot m_{\text{exp}} &\approx 0.20099~\text{kg/s}, \\
          \dot m_{\text{man}} &\approx 0.15470~\text{kg/s}, \\
          \dot m_{\text{obs}} &\approx 0.16213~\text{kg/s}.
        \end{align*}
  \item KM and MC 95\% half--widths (instrument--only model) are roughly:
        \begin{align*}
          U_{95,\text{exp}} &\approx \pm 0.018~\text{kg/s}
             \quad (\text{about } 9\%),\\
          U_{95,\text{man}} &\approx \pm 5\times10^{-5}~\text{kg/s}
             \quad (\text{very small}),\\
          U_{95,\text{obs}} &\approx \pm 0.0017~\text{kg/s}
             \quad (\text{about } 1\%).
        \end{align*}


\end{itemize}
\section{Discussion}
\subsection*{Methods}
Before the execution of the experiment, ambient data is taken. The procedure in lab 1 is then followed which will not be discussed here.
Safety protocol should be followed and operation of the HVAC system is handled by appropriate personnel.
Important considerations are sources of uncertainty in the methodology: Voltage dial, current dial, mass flow rate measurement, etc.
These sources of uncertainties are summarized in table 1 which fulfills the first task.
\\~\\
The pitot static tube data at the orifice on different radii and the data from psychrometric chart are handled in R to calculate mass flows
as demanded by the objective. Statistical uncertainties are calculated in R using KM and a structure shown in lecture for MC.


\subsection*{Analysis}
The pitot-statc probe measures dynamic pressures at each radius expressed as: $ \Delta p(r) = P_0(r) - P(r) $.
Since the speed of the flow is low, incompressible flow theory can be used to calculate the velocity: $
           V(r) = \sqrt{\frac{2 \Delta p(r)}{\rho}}. $. Moist air is modeled with ideal gas assumption as a mixture of dry air and water vapor: $ E_s(T_{wb}) = 6.112
                   \exp\!\left(
                     \frac{17.67\,T_{wb}}{T_{wb} + 243.5}
                   \right) \times 100
                   \; \text{Pa}. $, $ P_v = E_s(T_{wb}) -
                   \gamma P_{\text{atm}}
                   \bigl(T_{db} - T_{wb}\bigr) $.
Mass flow is calculated using a (1)trapz version in R of the following inegral: $ \dot m = \iint_A \rho\,V\,dA. $,
(2) Manufacturer's correlation: $ \dot m_{\text{man}} = C \sqrt{\frac{z}{v}},
           \qquad C = 0.0504 $, and 
(3)$ \dot m_{\text{obs}} = \rho Q
           = C_d E A_2 \sqrt{2\rho \Delta p}. $
\\~\\
KM and MC 95\% intervals are very similar for all three mass flow
estimates. However, both KM and MC only propagate the uncertainties that are
explicitly included (thermometers, manometers, geometry). Some missing
systematic components (e.g.\ in \(C_d\), \(C\), pitot coefficient)
likely explain the lack of overlap between the mean values of
\( \dot m_{\text{exp}} \), \( \dot m_{\text{man}} \), and
\( \dot m_{\text{obs}} \).

\section*{Closing and Recommendation}

The velocity profile integrations has the most experimental errors. Manufacturer's correlation is the best in terms of uncertainty.
Take more radial pitot measurements (especially near the orifice edge)
to reduce numerical integration error in \( \dot m_{\text{exp}} \). Repeat measurements on all aspects of the experiment is a good improvement as well.

\section*{Appendix: Summary of Key Equations}

\subsection*{Thermo–Fluid Relations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Saturation vapor pressure at wet–bulb (Tetens fit, \(T\) in \(^\circ\text{C}\)):
    $$
      E_s(T_{wb}) =
      6.112 \exp\!\left(
        \frac{17.67\,T_{wb}}{T_{wb} + 243.5}
      \right)\times 100
      \;\text{Pa}
    $$
  \item Psychrometric wet–bulb relation for actual vapor pressure:
    $$
      P_v = E_s(T_{wb})
            - \gamma P_{\text{atm}}
              \bigl(T_{db} - T_{wb}\bigr)
    $$
  \item Dalton's law for dry–air partial pressure:
    $$
      P_d = P_{\text{atm}} - P_v
    $$
  \item Moist–air density from ideal gas law for each component:
    $$
      \rho_d = \frac{P_d}{R_d T_{db}},
      \qquad
      \rho_v = \frac{P_v}{R_v T_{db}},
      \qquad
      \rho = \rho_d + \rho_v
      = \frac{P_d}{R_d T_{db}} + \frac{P_v}{R_v T_{db}}
    $$
  \item Specific volume:
    $$
      v = \frac{1}{\rho}
    $$
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Velocity and Mass Flow}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Pitot–static velocity (incompressible Bernoulli):
    $$
      \Delta p(r) = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho V(r)^2
      \quad \Rightarrow \quad
      V(r) = \sqrt{\frac{2\Delta p(r)}{\rho}}
    $$
  \item Ideal definition of mass flow:
    $$
      \dot m = \iint_A \rho V\,dA
    $$
  \item Axisymmetric plane, annular area element:
    $$
      dA = 2\pi r\,dr,
      \qquad
      d\dot m = \rho V(r)\,2\pi r\,dr
    $$
  \item Trapezoidal approximation over slices \(i\):
    $$
      V_{\text{avg},i} = \frac{V_i + V_{i+1}}{2},\quad
      r_{\text{avg},i} = \frac{r_i + r_{i+1}}{2},\quad
      \Delta r_i = r_{i+1} - r_i
    $$
    $$
      \delta \dot m_i \approx
      \rho V_{\text{avg},i} 2\pi r_{\text{avg},i} \Delta r_i,
      \qquad
      \dot m_{\text{exp}} \approx \sum_i \delta \dot m_i
    $$
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Manufacturer Correlation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Given correlation (with \(z\) in mm H\(_2\)O, \(v\) in m\(^3\)/kg):
    $$
      \dot m_{\text{man}} = C \sqrt{\frac{z}{v}},
      \qquad C = 0.0504
    $$
  \item Using \(v = 1/\rho\):
    $$
      \dot m_{\text{man}} = C \sqrt{z \rho}
    $$
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Orifice (Obstruction–Meter) Theory}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Beta ratio and velocity–of–approach factor:
    $$
      \beta = \frac{d}{D},
      \qquad
      E = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^4}}
    $$
  \item Orifice area:
    $$
      A_2 = \frac{\pi d^2}{4}
    $$
  \item Volumetric flow rate and mass flow rate:
    $$
      Q = C_d E A_2 \sqrt{\frac{2\Delta p}{\rho}},
      \qquad
      \dot m_{\text{obs}} = \rho Q
      = C_d E A_2 \sqrt{2\rho \Delta p}
    $$
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Kline–McClintock (KM) Uncertainty}

\begin{itemize}
  \item General KM formula for a result
    \( r = f(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_N) \):
    $$
      u_r^2 \approx
      \sum_{i=1}^{N}
      \left(
        \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i} u_{x_i}
      \right)^2
    $$
  \item 95\% uncertainty interval (Gaussian assumption):
    $$
      r \pm 1.96\,u_r
    $$
  \item Relative form for multiplicative power laws,
    \( r = K \prod x_i^{a_i} \):
    $$
      \left(\frac{u_r}{r}\right)^2
      \approx
      \sum_i
      \left(
        a_i \frac{u_{x_i}}{x_i}
      \right)^2
    $$
  \item Slice mass flow
    \( \delta \dot m_i = \rho V_i 2\pi r_i \Delta r_i \)
    (approximate relative uncertainty):
    $$
      \left(
        \frac{u_{\delta \dot m_i}}{\delta \dot m_i}
      \right)^2
      \approx
      \left(\frac{u_\rho}{\rho}\right)^2
      +
      \left(\frac{u_{V_i}}{V_i}\right)^2
      +
      \left(\frac{u_r}{r_i}\right)^2
    $$
  \item Manufacturer correlation
    \( \dot m_{\text{man}} = C (z\rho)^{1/2} \):
    $$
      \left(
        \frac{u_{\dot m_{\text{man}}}}{\dot m_{\text{man}}}
      \right)^2
      \approx
      \frac{1}{4}
      \left[
        \left(\frac{u_z}{z}\right)^2
        +
        \left(\frac{u_\rho}{\rho}\right)^2
      \right]
    $$
  \item Orifice theory
    \( \dot m_{\text{obs}} \propto A (\rho z)^{1/2} \),
    with \( A \propto D^2 \) and \( u_A/A \approx 2 u_D/D \):
    $$
      \left(
        \frac{u_{\dot m_{\text{obs}}}}{\dot m_{\text{obs}}}
      \right)^2
      \approx
      \left(\frac{u_A}{A}\right)^2
      +
      \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{u_\rho}{\rho}\right)^2
      +
      \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{u_z}{z}\right)^2
    $$
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Monte Carlo (MC) Uncertainty}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Model each input \(x_i\) as a normal random variable:
    $$
      x_i^{(m)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\bar x_i, u_{x_i})
    $$
  \item For each replicate \(m = 1,\dots,M\):
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item Draw random inputs \(x_i^{(m)}\).
      \item Evaluate the data reduction equation
            $$ r^{(m)} = f\bigl(x_1^{(m)},x_2^{(m)},\dots,x_N^{(m)}\bigr). $$
    \end{enumerate}
  \item Use the empirical distribution of
    \( \{r^{(m)}\} \) to estimate uncertainty;  
    the 95\% interval is typically taken as the 2.5\% and 97.5\%
    quantiles of \(r^{(m)}\).
\end{itemize}
