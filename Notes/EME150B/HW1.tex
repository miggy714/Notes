    \item A spur gearset has a pitch of 4 teeth/in and a speed ratio of 1/4. The pinion has 16 teeth.
    Find the number of teeth on the gear, the pitch diameters, and the theoretical center-to-
    center distance.
    \\
    Solution: center-to-center-distance is between 6 – 12 in
    \\~\\
    Center-tocenter distance can be expressed as: $\frac{d_{pinion}+d_{gear}}{2}$
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Diametral Pitch $\equiv P =\frac{N}{d} =[\frac{teeth}{in}]$ 
        \begin{itemize}
            \item $P_{gear}=4$
            \item $P_{pinion}=?$ by rule 2: $P_{gear}=P_{pinion}$
            \item $N_{pinion} = 16$
        \end{itemize}
        \item Speed Ratio $\equiv S_{ratio} = \frac{n_{out}}{n_{in}} = \frac{d_{pinion}}{d_{gear}}=1/4$
    \end{itemize}
    diameter of pinion can be expressed as: $d_{pinion} = d_{gear}/4$ (I)
    \\
    by rule 2: $P_{gear}=\frac{N_{pinion}}{d_{pinion}}$ (II)
    \\
    combining eq. (I) and (II):
    \begin{align*}
        \frac{N_{pinion}}{P_{gear}}&=d_{gear}/4
        \\
        d_{gear} &= 4(16/4) = 16 in
    \end{align*}
    Speed ratio can be used to calculate $d_{pinion}$: $d_{pinion}=16/4=4in$
    \\~\\
    \fbox{\textbf{Answer: Center-to-center distance $= \frac{d_{pinion}+d_{gear}}{2} = \frac{20}{2} = 10in$}}






    \item Explain the difference between
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item internal and external gearsets
        \\
        \textbf{Internal gearsets involve a ponion and a gear resembling a ring with the teeth on the inside. External gearsets are the traditional gears mated with teeth on the external perimeter.}
        \item simple and compound gear trains
        \\
        \textbf{Simple gear trains involve only one gear per shaft and can easily be modelled with $n_k = \frac{N_{k-1}}{N_{k}} \frac{N_{k-2}}{N_{k-1}} \dots \frac{N_1}{N_2}n_1$ where n is angular speed rad/sec. Compound gear trains have two gears on a shaft which means these gears have the same angular velocity.}
    \end{enumerate}
    








    \item Design a compound, 2 stage reverted spur-gear train for an overall train value of 1:20. The
    module is 4 mm, gear 2 has 23 teeth and gear 5 has 80 teeth.
    \\~\\
    Governing equations and assumptions:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Remember train value denoted as e: $e = \frac{\text{Product of driving tooth numbers}}{\text{Product of driven tooth numbers}}$ 
        
        \item Note: $n_k = \frac{N_{k-1}}{N_{k}} \frac{N_{k-2}}{N_{k-1}} \dots \frac{N_1}{N_2}n_1$ where n is angular speed rad/sec
        
        \item A 2 stage reverted spur-gear train has a total of 4 total gears declared as gear 2 (driving gear), gear 3 and 4 (equal angular velocity), and gear 5 (Output)
        \item A 2 stage reverted spur-gear is constrained geometrically by having the input and output shafts in-line: $d_2+d_3=d_4+d_5$
    \end{enumerate}
    Answers to questions:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item What are the teeth numbers of gears 3 and 4?
        \\
        express e in terms of gears in gear train:
        \begin{align*}
            n_2 &= \frac{N_3}{N_2}n_3
            \\
            n_4 &= \frac{N_5}{N_4}n_5
        \end{align*}
        Since gear 3 and 4 have equal angular velocity:
            $$n_2 = \frac{N_3N_5}{N_2N_4}n_5 \rightarrow n_5 = \frac{N_2N_4}{N_3N_5}n_2 \rightarrow e = \frac{N_2N_4}{N_3N_5}$$
        So far, 2 unknowns. Need one more equation, the geometric constraint:
        \\
        Also, $m = d/N$
        \begin{align*}
            d_2+d_3&=d_4+d_5
            \\
            N_2+N_3&=N_4+N_5
        \end{align*}
        2 equations, 2 unknowns:
        \begin{enumerate}
            \item $N_2+N_3=N_4+N_5$
            \item $e = \frac{N_2N_4}{N_3N_5}$
        \end{enumerate}
        \fbox{\textbf{Answer: }$N_3$ = 69 teeth, $N_4$ = 12 teeth}
        \item Please give the pitch diameters of all four gears.
        \begin{align*}
            d &= mN
            \\
            d_2 &= 0.004(N_2) = 92 mm
            \\
            d_3 &= 0.004(N_3) = 276 mm
            \\
            d_4 &= 0.004(N_4) = 48 mm
            \\
            d_5 &= 0.004(N_5) = 320 mm  
        \end{align*}

        \item If gear 2 has an input torque of 2 Nm, how much torque does gear 4 transmit?
        \begin{enumerate}
            \item Force is transmitted perfectly
            \item $T = F*d/2$
            \item $N = d/m$
        \end{enumerate}
        using (b) and (c): $T = \frac{FNm}{2}$
        \\
        \begin{align*}
            T_2 &= 2 = \frac{FN_2m}{2}
            \\
            F &= \frac{1}{N_2m} = \frac{1}{23(0.004)} = 10.87 \text{N}
        \end{align*}
        \fbox{\textbf{Answer: }Torque of gear 4: $T_4 = \frac{FN_4m}{2} = \frac{10.87(12)(0.004)}{2} =  0.26 \text{Nm}$}
        \end{itemize}
